# YUANEN editorial publishing

This site is a static, trilingual React/Vite build. Published articles are TypeScript files in `src/articles/`; adding one file creates all three localized detail pages during prerendering. Keep drafts outside that directory. Do not create a `/blog/` route.

## Sections and cadence

- Product and purchasing articles: `/guides/{slug}/`, listed under the existing `/guides/` page.
- Industry updates: `/industry-news/{slug}/`, listed under `/industry-news/`. The industry index is generated with the first publishable article; an empty index is not published.
- Archive pagination begins after 12 new articles in a section. Existing guide URLs and cards stay in place.
- As confirmed on 2026-10-12, scheduled runs begin at 09:00 for up to three purchasing guides and 15:00 for up to two industry updates, Asia/Shanghai. One independent topic with three localized pages counts as one article. Updates to an existing page, translations, drafts and unverified deployments do not count toward the daily total. Do not repeat completed topics or backfill missed dates.
- Work through due tasks, eligible unlocked later tasks without date dependencies, then independently evidenced topics based on verified live products or recent first-party events. Record a rejected topic and continue to the next; a single duplicate, missing metric or unsuitable cover does not end the run. Fix or regenerate an unsuitable new cover rather than substitute an old image. Stop only for a genuine shared blocker or after checking the queue and relevant alternative directions without finding publishable material.
- Complete and verify each article before continuing to the next, including its three production URLs. Preserve workbook locks, stable IDs, user-selected dates and completed records. Publishing does not authorize new products or additional paid research.

## Article file contract

Export one `EditorialArticle` as the default value of a new `src/articles/{slug}.ts` file. The type lives in `src/editorial.ts`. Use a distinct lowercase-hyphenated slug and fill every `en`, `zh`, and `es` field with a genuine translation. Required fields include `kind`, `slug`, `publishedAt`, `title`, `description`, `intro`, at least one `section`, at least one verifiable `source`, and one relevant image. Use `relatedProducts` only for existing product slugs. For industry updates, also supply `eventDate`, three-language `market`, and at least one source with `publishedAt`. Generate a new article-specific illustration rather than reuse a site product image as the article's main visual. Save a web-optimized landscape image under `public/images/`, include it in responsive image preparation, and provide accurate three-language alt text and captions. Use the localized Illustration label. Disclose AI generation on the linked /content-policy/ page, not repeatedly in every card or caption; never present it as a photo of a real product, facility, test, funded purchase or finished order. Check the rendered list and detail pages, including mobile crop, before publication. If a suitable new image cannot be generated and checked, skip publication rather than publish a text-only article or reuse an old image.

For Google image discovery, keep the main image in a crawlable `<img src>` (not only a CSS background), provide responsive sizes and real dimensions, use a descriptive filename, and make the visible image, alt text and caption agree. The article's absolute `Article.image` URL and `og:image` must point to that same image. Prefer a high-resolution, web-optimized file and check that the page and image are not blocked from indexing in production. Google recommends 16:9, 4:3 and 1:1 high-resolution variants for the best Article structured-data results; these are recommendations, not a promise of image or rich-result display. See [Google Images best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images) and [Article structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article).

For editorial covers, use a coherent visual related to the article's topic and section, not a literal assembly of objects or events mentioned in the copy. A buying guide may show the relevant generic packaging type or material; industry news may show a related industry setting without pretending to document the reported event. Let the subject fill the frame. Reject large empty margins, obvious split-scene composites, glossy mockup surfaces, implausible bag construction, and mismatched background panels. Check the image at its actual desktop and mobile display size. As confirmed on 2026-10-12, list and related-article cards do not display a separate Illustration label. Detail figures retain the localized illustration label and factual caption; articles link to the three-language content-policy disclosure. Keep accurate alt text and internal generation/source/rights records, even when the style resembles a photograph.

## Titles

Use natural, varied titles grounded in the actual article. A guide should identify a specific purchasing decision; an industry headline should identify the real institution, event or change. Do not repeatedly generate product-name plus “Fit:” plus a three-item list, or replace that pattern with another universal question or “How to” template. A colon is allowed when it serves the title, not required or globally forbidden. Keep keyword meaning without stacking near-synonyms. Keep the displayed title, H1, archive and related-card titles, document title, Open Graph metadata and Article headline synchronized through article data. Title-only revisions preserve URLs, publication dates, body facts, sources and image provenance, and do not count as new articles.

The source list is displayed on the page. Do not invent studies, search volumes, rankings, reviews, prices, delivery promises, certifications, or tests. A cited external report is not proof that YUANEN's products passed its tests. Tie product claims to the site's actual catalog and specify what a buyer still needs to confirm.

As confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-08, Google Trends is a research priority rather than a publication gate. Start with real catalog products, useful buyer questions, existing coverage and available GSC data, then inspect recent related and Rising queries in Google Trends Explore (prefer the last 90 days) for the relevant market. Research product families and actual applications rather than a few generic seeds. Record the seed query, any selected term, region, window, check date, result and Explore link or screenshot/export in the run report. Check ambiguity, seasonality and buying intent before using a rising long-tail naturally in the title, description and body. Trends reports relative interest, not search volume, ranking or purchase intent. If Trends is unavailable or no relevant rising term is found, record that limitation and continue only when the article has independently verifiable value; do not call the topic popular or rising. GSC unavailability alone also does not block publication. Guides need a specific, non-duplicate purchasing decision grounded in the site's products. Industry updates still require a recent first-party event source, date, market and a concrete implication for packaging buyers. Reject a topic with insufficient evidence or failed checks and continue the queue under the cadence rules above; do not fabricate facts to meet a quota.

## Publication checks

`dev` and `build` regenerate `src/editorial-index.json` from the published article files. Do not edit that index by hand. Routes and archives use only its metadata; the browser loads each article body as a separate module. Server rendering still reads and validates complete articles and emits their full body, sources and schema into static HTML. Performance verification checks index freshness, separate article modules and page-specific body preloads while retaining the existing script-size budgets.

Run `pnpm run build` and `pnpm run verify` before committing. The checks cover localized route generation, canonical/hreflang, article schema and visible dates, source and product links, archive membership, sitemap, image presence, and the established site regressions. A failed check blocks publication. Before pushing, check the remote branch and push only a fast-forward commit to `main`. Verify the Vercel production deployment and the live localized URLs; a successful Git push alone is not deployment proof. Report any skipped slot or failed deployment explicitly.

The scheduled workflow is AI-assisted; /content-policy/ explains this in three languages and article sources link to it. Visible article notes retain source and sample-confirmation boundaries without repeating AI labels. Preserve image-generation provenance and any existing source metadata. The owner explicitly chose automatic publication without per-article human approval; do not describe these articles as human reviewed.

## Commercial source links — owner clarification, 2026-10-31

Competitor and commercial supplier pages used for SERP or writing research stay in the internal automation evidence directory. Do not automatically publish these research URLs in a purchasing guide. Use verified YUANEN product records and relevant site guides for product scope and related reading. Retain a necessary original research citation when the claim itself must remain. Official regulatory guidance, applicable standards descriptions and carrier rules may remain when the guide uses them.
